# Deliverable structures

Use these as defaults only after applying the current official template and rubric.

## Competition brief

Maintain one source record containing: problem, target user, current alternatives, solution, innovation, technical status, validation, market, competitors, business model, impact, implementation plan, team, IP, risks, financial assumptions, evidence IDs, and rubric mapping.

## Business plan or application

1. Executive summary and competition fit
2. Problem and beneficiaries/customers
3. Solution, product, and technical feasibility
4. Innovation and defensibility
5. Validation and traction
6. Market and competitive landscape
7. Business model or impact model
8. Implementation, operations, and milestones
9. Team and governance
10. Financial model and funding/resource use
11. Risks, ethics, compliance, and mitigations
12. Evidence appendix and references

## Pitch deck

Default narrative: opening claim → problem → solution/demo → innovation → evidence/traction → market → competition → business/impact model → roadmap → team → finance/resource ask → closing. Keep one main claim per slide and place evidence IDs in notes or a source appendix.

## Financial model

Include assumptions, scenario selector, revenue drivers, direct costs, operating expenses, cash flow/runway, break-even logic, unit economics where applicable, and sensitivity analysis. Separate historical actuals from forecasts visually and structurally.

## Evidence appendix

Use an indexed table: evidence ID, claim supported, source/date, verification state, permission status, and file/link. Prefer a small set of strong, legible evidence over decorative screenshot walls.

## Defense package

Prepare a timed script, slide-by-slide speaker notes, demo fallback, role allocation, and Q&A bank. Questions should cover innovation, feasibility, competitors, market evidence, IP, finance, team, risks, social value, and discrepancies a judge may notice.

## Poster and visuals

Derive text and numbers from the competition brief. Keep diagrams editable when possible, label generated concept art, and never depict an unbuilt prototype as a deployed product without a clear concept/prototype label.
