# Defense red-team protocol

## Question design

Generate questions from evidence gaps and contradictions, not from a generic list. Cover at least the relevant categories:

- problem severity and target-user definition;
- technical mechanism, maturity, reproducibility, and limitations;
- comparison baseline and why alternatives are insufficient;
- user research, pilots, retention, conversion, and negative findings;
- market definition, pricing, cost, unit economics, and cash assumptions;
- IP ownership, freedom to operate, data rights, privacy, safety, and compliance;
- team roles, student contribution, dependencies, milestones, and execution risk;
- social value, impact measurement, unintended effects, and scale constraints.

## Answer record

For every high-risk question maintain:

`question_id | judge concern | concise answer | evidence_id | slide/demo anchor | limitation | follow-up owner`

Use a three-part answer where suitable: direct conclusion, evidence, boundary or next test. Never bluff. If evidence is missing, state what is known, what is not, and the concrete verification plan.

## Rehearsal passes

1. **Timing pass:** complete within 85–90% of the official limit so interruption recovery is possible.
2. **Evidence pass:** every number, comparison, partner, IP, and traction statement has a retrievable source.
3. **Hostile pass:** challenge the strongest claimed advantage and the weakest assumption.
4. **Interruption pass:** answer out of sequence and return to the narrative cleanly.
5. **Failure pass:** test offline demo, video, font, adapter, network, and presenter fallbacks.

Record failed answers as project gaps or communication gaps. Project gaps require evidence or execution; wording alone cannot close them.
