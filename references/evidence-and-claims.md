# Evidence and claims policy

## Evidence states

| State | Meaning | Allowed wording |
|---|---|---|
| Verified | Inspectable source supports the claim | State precisely and cite evidence ID |
| User-asserted | User reports it, but proof is not inspected | Attribute to the project team; request proof |
| Planned/forecast | Future action or modeled result | Use future/conditional language and assumptions |
| Missing | Needed claim has no support | Mark as a gap; do not publish as fact |

## Claim-to-evidence ledger

For each material claim record:

`claim_id | exact claim | state | evidence_id/source | owner | date | deliverables using it | notes`

Evidence IDs should point to stable files, URLs, screenshots, certificates, datasets, experiment logs, contracts, or signed letters. Preserve originals; do not edit source evidence to fit the story.

## Common distinctions

- Patent application ≠ granted patent; software registration submission ≠ issued certificate.
- Letter of intent ≠ contract; contract ≠ paid order; GMV ≠ revenue; revenue ≠ profit.
- Interview interest ≠ signup; signup ≠ active use; pilot ≠ repeat adoption.
- Lab test ≠ field validation; demo ≠ production deployment.
- Team target ≠ historical result; forecast ≠ audited financial statement.
- Media mention ≠ organizer award or third-party endorsement.

## Quantitative claims

Every number needs a definition, period, unit, denominator where relevant, and source. Financial forecasts must expose price, volume, conversion, retention, cost, hiring, tax, and cash assumptions. Market-size estimates must state method and avoid mixing incompatible sources.

## Privacy and authorization

Remove unnecessary personal data. Obtain permission before publishing partner names, logos, customer quotations, faces, contracts, or confidential technical details. Redact evidence copies when the competition permits it and keep an authorized original separately.
